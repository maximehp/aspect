# Transport in the playable room

The room uses one point lamp, two-sided finite sheets, convex filleted solids and matte RGB materials. A pose determines the entire solution. Worker allocations and compiled formulas survive an edit. Every visibility relation, integration weight, support function and light coefficient is built from the new geometry.

## What passes between surfaces

On each authored chart the incident field is a sum of a lamp term and an indirect term. The lamp term retains the light position, intensity and occluder geometry. A planar source can carry a bright stripe between two dark corners because the next reflection integrates the visible portion of the source itself. The indirect term uses local bilinear functions. Each side of a resolved physical contact has its own trace. Unobstructed adjoining regions share coefficients. A material scales their sum by its diffuse reflectance divided by π to obtain outgoing radiance. The engine stores π-scaled radiance for observation and places the factor of 1/π in its geometric weights.

For every receiver point the geometric kernel multiplies visibility by the two positive facing cosines and inverse squared distance. The source integral clips generator intervals with both emitter and receiver visibility, unites overlapping occlusions, and evaluates the remaining contributions. Eligible clear near-field apertures go to the GPU. The lamp and smooth source terms use the same receiver-area measure. [Source integration](src/function-transport.c) · [GPU integration](src/angular-measure.js)

Each surface starts with its natural analytic charts. Nearby parallel surfaces can introduce initial breaks from their geometry. The engine measures the difference between a candidate field and a fresh application of the integral, then splits failing domains along the direction of variation. Adjacent leaves have a shared trace, including hanging nodes and the seams of fillets. Nine interior Gauss observations per leaf integrate against the four local basis functions and the physical area Jacobian. They assemble a consistent mass matrix. Solving this system reconstructs the reflected function; cell averages and point samples do not serve as emitted light. A positivity constraint preserves the integrated incident RGB on each connected material side. The fixed-point iteration starts with current-pose lamp input and stops at an absolute coefficient residual of 0.000025. [Surface functions](src/surface-functions.js) · [Common receiver and equilibrium](src/field-equilibrium.js)

For the lamp → slat → wall → floor path, isolate the lamp term emitted by a slat, apply the common receiver projection, then reflect the wall component of that field onto the floor. The wall also emits the directly lit part of its own surface, bounded by the slat silhouette. Each branch has a distinct path history. Both continue from floor to ceiling through the same operator. The browser audit returns the lit intervals in wall coordinates, both floor contributions, and their next reflection. [Handoff audit](scripts/trace-room.js)

The camera classifies horizontal pixel runs using the same authored solids and finite lamp-segment intersections. The GPU evaluates the exact chart coordinate of the visible point and looks up the same represented indirect field that fed equilibrium. Screen geometry uses line primitives. [Visibility](src/fillet-kernel.c) · [Field evaluation on screen](src/function-display.js)

## What the wall hands the floor

For a point lamp and a rounded solid wholly between the lamp and an authored plane, perspective projects the eight corner balls to ellipses. The finite tangent generators join their visible arcs. Their projected endpoints determine where the two exposed features change. Each transverse span stores its two endpoint functions. Ellipse sections have the form $x=x_0+m(t-t_0)\pm a\sqrt{1-((t-t_0)/b)^2}$. Adjacent spans with the same owners share one record. Every receiver using this wall reads the same pose-wide shadow. Silhouettes straddling the observer or receiver plane and ambiguous section endpoints use the finite-segment geometry. Overlapping blockers contribute their interval union. [Rounded outline](src/fillet-footprint.js) · [Shadow union](src/plane-shadow.js)

For a finite sheet such as the bench seat, write $h,U,V$ for signed height and two sheet coordinates. The source and receiver each supply an affine function for each coordinate. Let $X$ be on the wall and $Y$ on the floor. Opposite heights imply a crossing. The edge $U=1$ at the crossing satisfies $h(Y) U(X)-h(X) U(Y)=h(Y)-h(X)$. The other three edges use the other sign and coordinate. These equations retain **both** wall and floor position. The sign of the inequalities follows the sign of the height difference. Binding one wall position therefore gives four lines on the floor. Binding a floor observation instead gives the precise interval limits needed to integrate the wall. A contact line where a sheet enters a receiver can disconnect its field. The implemented independent traces cover axis-aligned finite chart contacts. [Sheet coordinates](src/sheet-boundary.js) · [Finite contact traces](src/plane-contact.js)

The local kernel still needs receiver cubature where the shared expansion fails its distance/error bound, the surfaces cross a facing horizon, or the region visibility proof is inconclusive. These are limits of the current reduction. Once the source coefficients are assembled, integrate each receiver test function $b_i$ once, giving $G_{ij}=\sum_q w_q b_i(q)K_j(q)$. Every following bounce acts on these moments. The original pointwise rows also provide a spatial residual for the settled field. Both the observer and the next emitter evaluate that same reconstructed function. The handoff audit reports the actual room's wall-to-floor classifications. [Receiver operator](src/field-equilibrium.js) · [Handoff](scripts/trace-room.js)

Exact full-edge neighbours with identical matte properties can form one authored plane for transport and display. The join requires representable coordinates and preserves the mapping from each authored object to its compiled surface. A pose or material edit re-evaluates the join. ## Error and limits

Source quadrature targets 0.0002. The spatial estimator tests each reflected channel against 0.0015 + 0.045 × its measured magnitude and currently allows four passes. The returned history records how many cells still fail. The fixed-point residual measures convergence within the chosen space. It cannot bound missed spatial features. Receiver quadrature, source-integral depth limits, and basis approximation still require independent error checks. A positivity correction can redistribute local light within a connected part of the measured field. Instrumented decompositions use the active set of the settled solution, so individual projected branches can have signed coefficients. Compare finite-area integrals with path-traced RGB. ## A point lamp on a cylindrical fillet

At fixed angle $\theta$, write the cylinder generator as $p(x)=p_0+x e$, $|e|=1$. The normal is independent of $x$. On a visible, lit and facing interval, the source cosine numerator for each of the lamp and receiver is constant. The receiving cosine numerator is $c+m x$. Let $l,y_e$ be the projected axial coordinates of lamp and receiver, and $L,Y$ their respective **squared** distances from the generator line. Apart from a constant factor including radius, the integrand is

$$ (c+m x)Q_l(x)^{-3/2}Q_y(x)^{-2},\qquad Q_l=(x-l)^2+L,\quad Q_y=(x-y_e)^2+Y. $$

A local analytic primitive avoids sampling along $x$. About interval midpoint $x_0$, use $z=(x-x_0)/h$, with $h$ its half-length, $U=Q_l/Q_l(x_0)$ and $W=Q_y/Q_y(x_0)$. Set $g=U^{-3/2}W^{-2}$. Its Taylor coefficients follow from $g(0)=1$ and $(UW)g'=(-\tfrac32 U'W-2UW')g$. With $g_{-1}=0$, a primitive is

$$F(x)=\frac{h}{Q_l(x_0)^{3/2}Q_y(x_0)^2}\sum_{k\ge0}
\frac{(c+m x_0)g_k+mh g_{k-1}}{k+1}z^{k+1}.$$

Evaluate $F(b)-F(a)$ at visible endpoints, sum over openings and integrate over $\theta$. The implementation splits a long generator until both complex pole distances exceed $3h$ and bounds the remaining series using Cauchy’s estimate on $|z|=2$. For bilinear outgoing light on a generator, only the moments of $1,x,x^2$ over $Q_y^2$ remain. Their endpoint formulas use rational functions and an angle difference. Spherical octants retain their curved meridians and area Jacobian. Their remaining integrals and the outer strip integral use deterministic embedded estimates. The per-pair absolute budget is $2\times10^{-4}$ and the outer relative target is 0.002. Refinement exhaustion is counted. These targets apply locally and do not certify every pixel or all visibility topology. [Length primitive and integration](src/function-transport.c)


## Arithmetic and geometry

Text such as `"0.1"` and `"1/10"` describes the same rational length. Numeric inputs retain the binary value actually passed. Slat radii, centers and half-extents preserve their authored definitions. Rotation axes calculated with sine and cosine carry the precision of the pose computation.

For a fillet sphere, write $Q$ for the light position relative to its center and $d$ for the travel vector. The tangent equation is $(Q\cdot d)^2-(Q\cdot Q-r^2)(d\cdot d)=0$. For a cylindrical edge, first project both vectors perpendicular to its axis. The axial extent, the sphere octant or cylinder quadrant, and the finite light segment select the physically relevant roots. Substituting a position on a wall gives an implicit conic across that wall. Along a generator its root is identified by $Ax^2+2Bx+C=0$ and a branch. Its decimal expansion is unnecessary for a side test. Within a pose, a lamp-lit interval union belongs to the source. Identical generator queries can use it on multiple receivers. Unions with the receiving visibility are intersected before integration.

The same feature equation can be restricted to a whole receiver plane as $Q(u,v)=a u^2+2buv+c v^2+2du+2ev+f$. For a cylinder, projecting onto its cross section annihilates the axial direction. If a planar generator family translates exactly along that axis, $b=c=e=0$ in those coordinates. The same tangent roots in $u$ serve every transverse position $v$. The native kernel recognizes the exact axis alignments it can prove in the room. A pose-local table keys those roots by source, observer and feature. Every strip still checks the clipped interval, finite axial extent, quadrant and segment ordering. The spherical caps can restore dependence on $v$. A separate bound on $n\cdot(eye-center)$ over a feature’s allowed unit normals rejects whole features that cannot be tangent. Separation of the source from the solid also eliminates endpoint quadratics. [Exact conic restriction](src/silhouette.js) · [Native clipping](src/function-transport.c)

The kernel uses floating point for ordinary decisions. Near a suspected tangent it can reconstruct the lamp conic from rational authored quantities and exact binary inputs. The camera’s uncertain curved intersections compare candidate rational endpoints with quadratic roots by sign and squaring. Several geometric clipping and depth comparisons still use absolute epsilons. The renderer does not provide a universal certified predicate, a guaranteed downstream physical error or a per-pixel rounding certificate. For its discrete positive equilibrium it reports the residual and, when the infinity-norm contraction bound $q<1$ holds, an error bound of residual divided by $1-q$. ## Directional light and participating media

A diffuse material eliminates outgoing direction from its response. The room uses this specialization. Independent dielectric and volume operations preserve direction and throughput. Their composition with the playable equilibrium, a dusty room, street and pool remain open. The diffuse room does not load these operations. [Optical operations](src/optical-operations.js)
