## Required rendering direction

Future renderer work must use the CPU for scene updates, geometry and visibility decisions, and light transport. The CPU prepares a view description with the lighting needed to draw it. The GPU’s role is drawing the resulting pixels, including rasterizing coverage, interpolating supplied values and writing the framebuffer. This division applies to new effects, including subsurface scattering. C26 tracks bringing the existing room onto this architecture.

## Dusty rooms

**H12.** Let a developer give the air absorption and scattering properties. The same dust should soften the contrast of a distant wall, reveal light around a lamp and go dark when all emission stops. A plain room with a lamp and a cabinet is enough to try this. A window or blind is an optional way to make a shadow edge easy to see.

The current visibility compiler already constructs a 3D shadow volume per opaque planar blocker before clipping it to walls. Investigate using those volumes directly in the air. Along a sightline ending at the first visible surface, combine overlapping shadows and identify the intervals exposed to the lamp. Account for extinction through the whole dusty distance. Integrate the lamp's scattered contribution across each exposed interval, using a closed form where available or numerical integration for the point lamp's varying distance and scattering angle. Walking can reuse the world-space shadow boundaries. Dimming can scale the lamp's full contribution. Moving the cabinet changes visibility. [Volumetric shadow intervals](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/bce6beb1-7e68-47b1-a90a-0742fe0bca26)

Carry extinction on light paths between surfaces as well. Investigate how light reflected by the room illuminates the air and how scattered light reaches surfaces and other air. Agreement on direct shafts alone leaves that coupling open. For stirred dust, moving density puffs are a candidate with analytic extinction along a segment. Check the lingering disturbance after an object passes. In one walk-through, compare against a reference at matched quality and record update cost, peak memory and finished 4K frame time under H10. [Volumetric primitives for scattering media](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15425)

## Fur and hair at brushing distance and across a room

**H13.** Author one groom on the animal or scalp. Anchor fiber roots to its surface. Store guide curves for the shapes of tufts or locks, a repeatable rule for fiber positions, lengths, curl and taper within each group, and local edits where a stroke separates fibers. This gives each strand a stable identity without storing every curve in full. The game developer assigns the groom and supplies ordinary brush motion. The renderer chooses how much of the groom to resolve.

**Direct queries in close-up.** Give each fiber an address in a patch on the skin. Let `F(u, v, s)` give its position for root coordinates `(u, v)` and distance fraction `s` from root to tip. For a regular or bounded-jitter root layout, investigate mapping a pixel footprint or swept brush back into the patch to find the small set of root cells it can reach. Bound the whole fiber, including overhanging tips. Evaluate taper, tangent and visibility on the relevant portions of those fibers. A shared stroke can update a local deformation rule with zero displacement at the roots. An independently displaced hair can keep an override at its address, with bounds covering its new position. Track folds, overlapping locks and accumulated edits that make the lookup expensive. [On-demand lookup of regular and flyaway textile fibers](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.13230)

**Restricted feasibility check, 2026-11-19.** A one-off calculation defined 96 × 64 opaque fibers on a unit-spaced root grid. Each had length 9 and horizontal disk cross sections of radius 0.22. A stroke across the patch tilted the straight fibers along one axis by a row-dependent amount, up to six spacings at the tips, while keeping roots fixed and centerline lengths constant. For an overhead orthographic sightline, its lateral coordinate selects at most one row. From the shift at tip height and the disk width at that coordinate, rounding up gives the first possible fiber along the tilt direction. Across 2,048 sampled sightlines, direct lookup and exhaustive intersection agreed on every miss and on the identity and depth of all 573 hits. Only the reference enumerated the tuft. This checks first visibility for this restricted family. Filtered shading, arbitrary views, brushing contact and general deformation remain open.

**Fiber-count check, 2026-11-19.** On the same unit-square patch, try 400 × 250, 1,000 × 1,000 and 10,000 × 10,000 roots. Keep fiber length fixed, scale radius with spacing, and jitter each root along its row by up to a fifth of a cell. A stroke gives the row a shared lean. At each count, 100,000 first-visibility queries from overhead and two tilts along the rows examined at most two candidate hairs per sightline. The first 512 results per count agreed on hit identity and depth or miss with a scan of every hair in the possible row. These views allow direct row selection. Cross-row views, overlapping curls, pixel filtering, lighting and the cost of independent brush edits at these densities remain unverified.

**Curly coat, brush and orbit, 2026-11-19.** A standalone CPU experiment defined 100 million root addresses across one square metre, with jittered positions and varying curl phase, radius, turns and tip height. Each fiber has up to 24 linearly tapered segments defined by swept horizontal disks. A spherical brush swept 3.8 mm, checked 2,240 candidate roots for centerline contact and bent 918 fibers. On one M1 Pro core the edit and cache invalidation took 1.06 ms. Rebuilding the affected spatial cells for the next 128 × 80 view took 3.26 million candidate-root visits across 8,336 distinct roots. That first post-edit framebuffer took 62 ms. Each subsequent new cell builds a list of possible segment IDs on demand.

Across a full orbit of the brushed tuft at 128 × 80, the 100-million case averaged 44 distinct candidate hairs and 76 segment intersection attempts per pixel. Per-view 95th percentiles ranged from 91 to 137 hairs, with a measured maximum of 317. Median walk frame time was 27.4 ms. At 100,000 and one million roots in the same area with fixed fiber widths and curl dimensions, the respective means were 4.4 and 10.7 hairs and the median times were 10.1 and 13.1 ms. The same small brush contacted zero and seven hairs at those densities. Additional overhead and edge-on views were rendered. All 512 additional sightlines sampled over upper viewing angles agreed on first-hit identity or miss with exhaustive local candidate enumeration using the same segment equation.

**Failed real-time 4K check for this CPU implementation.** The 100-million brushed view took 14.82 s starting with empty rendering cells and 14.57 s on one warm repeat. These times cover one visibility sample per pixel and simple tangent shading through the in-memory framebuffer. Filtering, self-shadowing, multiple scattering, display and collisions between fibers remain outside the measurement. Next investigate reuse over a pixel footprint and across neighboring views. Verify any time savings at matched appearance including moving highlights and silhouette coverage. General curled or tangled grooms, persistent independent edits beyond this stroke, and a tuned baseline remain open.

**Across the room.** Investigate shading an unresolved clump as a curved bundle. Derive its projected coverage, distribution of fiber directions and response to light from its member strands. This should preserve the soft outline, moving sheen and light scattered between pale hairs. Two tufts brushed in opposite directions can have the same density and different highlights. Keep a parting, a rim-lit flyaway or a distinct lock separate whenever it affects the image. [Aggregate strand shading and level of detail](https://kuiwuchn.github.io/hairlod.html)

**At brush distance.** Reconstruct the same tapered curves from their roots and current guides wherever gaps, tips or overlapping strands become visible. Investigate integrating each curve's coverage over the pixel so thin hairs remain stable as the camera moves. Replace a bundle with smaller groups or its member fibers over the same footprint. Check that coverage, highlights and light scattered between fibers agree through the transition. Hair and animal fur can share this geometry while using different fiber optics, including scattering by the inner core of a fur fiber. [Fur and hair reflectance](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~lingqi/project_page/fur2/index.html)

**Brushing.** Use the swept brush shape to find the guides or strands it actually touches, including free ends of long hair. Bend them along the stroke while retaining their roots and lengths. If a stroke runs through the middle of a clump, allow the two sides to acquire separate guides so interpolation preserves the part. Store the change on the groom and update the affected bundle summaries. A stroke still visible from across the room should change the direction of the sheen there. **Related questions.** For long hair, check whether the same rules preserve overlapping locks and their independent motion. Fabric is a conditional extension. ## Side-chat leads for future brainstorming

The user's question is whether different internal rules can produce the same observable consequences for every allowed sequence of player actions. The window example combines walking past the opening, dimming the city lights, and breaking a chimney. A description of the light crossing the window must account for parallax, newly exposed surfaces, relighting, and the persistent effect of the edit in any allowed order.

These examples and reading leads were supplied in the side chats. Their combined generality, exactness, memory use, and speed remain unverified. The entries give context for the existing hypotheses and carry no new implementation decision.

- **Relighting and interaction (H06, H09, H10).** With fixed geometry, viewpoint, and linear optical materials, one fixed ambient image plus ten exact per-lamp contributions, including bounces, suffices for any combination of ten dimmers. Scale and sum in linear light before tone mapping. Moving a table can change the contributions. For a moving observer the response must also vary with viewpoint. Breaking the chimney requires enough persistent state to reproduce what later becomes visible. Reading lead: Debevec et al.'s reflectance-field work.
- **Deferred exact expressions and direct equilibrium (H03, H06).** Carry an irrational by its defining expression until the requested decision is known. The sign of a true quadratic's discriminant settles the existence of real roots without constructing them. Reading leads include CORE and CGAL's lazy exact arithmetic and predicates. In a scalar optical loop with emission E and retained fraction 0 ≤ r < 1, the equilibrium is E/(1−r). For fixed linear transport K, a factorization might let many light settings reuse the action of (I−K)⁻¹ where the inverse exists. Construction, storage, and updates after edits remain open costs.
- **Visibility across a region (H02, H03, H05).** Real quantifier elimination can remove the depth variable from suitable polynomial visibility conditions, although the resulting screen-space formula can grow sharply. For spheres with coplanar centers seen orthographically along the plane normal, comparisons of front depth give a power diagram clipped to the projected disks. Changing one center's depth can restore nonlinear boundaries. A depth gap of (u−v)² + 1/100 certifies a strict order over an entire patch. A candidate sum-of-squares certificate found numerically needs rigorous verification. Reading leads include *Real Quantifier Elimination in Practice*, §4.8, and *Sum-of-Squares Geometry Processing*.
- **Integrated detector response (H04, H07).** For the ideal fold x = u² with unit energy per unit u on both branches, the preimage of [0,h] has length 2√h for h ≥ 0. This gives the collected energy even where the pointwise density diverges. A proposed lens-and-receiver test would check conservation as a detector edge crosses a caustic. *Computing the Volume of Compact Semi-Algebraic Sets* is a reading lead for certified coverage of flat-colored curved silhouettes using Picard–Fuchs equations. Favorable scaling with requested precision assumes a fixed input. Reusing a differential equation across pixels or frames is speculative.
- **Measurements sufficient for a shape family (H04, C21).** For one nonempty uniformly white interval on black, its area gives its width and its first moment divided by area gives its midpoint. Those two measurements recover both endpoints. Two arbitrary disjoint intervals can share the same width sum and centroid. Reading leads include finite-rate-of-innovation recovery from selected Fourier measurements and moment-based order-independent transparency, whose general layer reconstruction is approximate.
- **Dense field compression (H06, H08).** Quantized tensor trains factor a grid using coordinate bits. Doubling linear resolution adds one bit per coordinate. The storage advantage depends on how large the internal matrix dimensions, or ranks, must become at the required accuracy. PuTT is a fitted, lossy visual-data lead. A proposed stress test goes from smooth indirect light to narrow moving stripes cast by rotating blinds, recording rank, error, memory, and update time.
- **Curved space and graded-index materials (H07, H09).** In the 2D geometric-optics model of Maxwell's fish-eye, a coordinate transformation represents light paths as great circles on a sphere. This suggests a shared propagation rule for selected optical materials and spaces. Surface intersections, boundary conditions, and the spreading of energy still need treatment. Reading leads include Maxwell fish-eye optics and interactive visualization of the eight Thurston geometries.

Reducing warm first-pose latency does not eliminate this settling period or the 4K long frames.

An isolated version that filled the omitted sites on the next frame matched the completed field exactly. During a 48-edit two-blind 4K comparison the median changed from 20.4 to 20.1 ms and p95 from 22.9 to 22.6 ms. A repeat over 96 edits in reverse test order changed median/p95 from 19.9/21.9 to 19.6/21.4 ms. Both versions delivered 96/96 edits. The small end-to-end gain did not justify a separate completion step and history state. The live renderer keeps the simpler full-field batch.
