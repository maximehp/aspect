# 3D vector engine

A walkable room with one point lamp and independently tiltable blinds. Run `npm start` and open [the room](http://127.0.0.1:8736/). The server uses Python 3. The browser needs WebAssembly and WebGL 2. There are no package dependencies.

Drag to look and use WASD to walk. The room opens with one slat. Change its fillet radius at a fixed angle, then enable **Animate slats** or add a second independently tilted slat. **Full blind** restores the nine-slat bank. Move the lamp slider to zero to darken the room. Choose **Reflected only** and switch the floor between red and cobalt to see the color of the floor on the plaster. The furniture layout is editable.

## Authoring the room

The room uses planar surfaces, rounded boxes and linear RGB material reflectances. Lengths are in metres. A rectangle has a center and two half-axes. A rounded box has a center, three orthonormal axes, three outer half-extents and a fillet radius. The single-slat control authors a 3.5 m × 340 mm × 90 mm solid with an adjustable 1–45 mm radius. The full blind uses 4 m × 226 mm × 24 mm slats with an initial 8 mm radius. The renderer derives its draw, shadow and transport representations from that solid. Shapes are immutable. Submit a replacement shape to move it or change its material. [Room definition](src/room.js) · [Rounded box](src/rounded-box.js)

## How a frame gets its light

The CPU computes the lamp-visible regions, diffuse equilibrium and camera visibility. The GPU integrates the visible polygons for contact lighting and caches stationary geometric coefficients. Each CPU scanline run identifies one visible surface and its point-lamp shadow state. The GPU draws the run as a horizontal line, evaluates that surface at pixel centers, applies the dimmer and display curve, and writes pixels. After an edit the first displayed frame already contains the solved field. The preceding path tracer remains available as a quality reference. [CPU renderer](src/room-display.js) · [Display geometry](src/cpu-view.js) · [Reference](src/live-renderer.js)

For fixed geometry, let D be the direct incident light, H the indirect incident light, ρ the surface's linear RGB reflectance, and K the diffuse transfer operator. An opaque diffuse scene obeys **H = K ρ(D + H)**. This accounts for light that reaches a surface after any number of reflections. The room approximates the spatial equation and solves for a mean and a slope on each side of each surface region. It stops when both coefficients satisfy a local update tolerance of 10⁻⁷ lamp units. The positivity limiter is homogeneous, so scaling the single lamp scales the unit-power result before display quantization. [Equilibrium](src/equilibrium.js)

- **Direct shadows.** On a planar receiver the CPU clips conservative shadow footprints for the incident-light integral. Where a rounded caster can let light past its outer envelope, it integrates the correction using segment intersections with the actual fillet. Screen scanlines use convex bounds to accept uniform runs and solve disputed endpoints on the authored rounded solid. These integrals and bounds are mathematical inputs. The room display submits horizontal lines. [Geometry and visibility kernel](src/fillet-kernel.c)
- **Rounded-surface exchange.** Surface quadrature evaluates the planar, cylindrical and spherical portions at their actual positions, areas and normals. The moving correction measures newly connected surfaces and blocked stationary exchange in 96 paired directions. It carries source and receiver spatial moments through the equilibrium solve. Angular and spatial quadrature limit its accuracy. The separate local contact field still clips conservative envelopes. [Surface quadrature](src/fillet-transport.js)
- **Variation in every reflection.** Each region carries RGB coefficients for a field of the form `a + bξ`, where ξ runs from −1 to 1 along one local tangent. The choice of tangent uses the solid face dimensions, lamp geometry and nearby sheet edges. For each visible pair, integrating 1, ξ at each endpoint, and their product gives four coefficients. These transfer a source gradient into both the mean and the gradient of the receiver. Positive endpoint bounds keep the reconstructed light nonnegative. This basis still loses variation across its second tangent and fine occlusion boundaries within an indirectly lit region. [CPU area sweep and solve](src/beam-sweep.c)
- **Near a parallel surface.** An empty-prism check enables the analytic mean form factor between aligned rectangles. Deterministic integration of that formula supplies the higher moments. For the last transfer the CPU can evaluate a clear rectangle's contribution at the receiver position. Planar surfaces share a continuous display field, with the same 48 × 24 samples per chart concentrated near geometric contacts. [Parallel-sheet transfer](src/parallel-transfer.js) · [Surface coordinates](src/irradiance-layout.js)
- **Where work disappears.** The stationary planar operator integrates visible area using 192 transverse strips and 96 paired directions. Its reuse avoids rebuilding the stationary scene whenever a slat moves. It stores sparse reciprocal links and reuses the stationary links. Moving a solid subtracts blocked stationary exchange and adds newly exposed interactions. A material-only edit reuses the geometric operator. Dimming uses one scalar, camera changes reuse equilibrium, and surface-side fields are reconstructed when their polygons enter the view. Up to six CPU workers share a geometry edit and their partial results are merged in a fixed order. Under rapid input the room keeps the newest waiting pose and presents each accepted pose with its lighting solved. [Operator storage](src/beam-exchange.js) · [Scene updates](src/room-display.js)

Storage for exchange follows the number of recorded interactions. A highly connected scene can still produce quadratically many links. Hierarchical compression is a remaining research question for the street, where large groups might share a smooth visible field. Occlusion can split a proposed group even when its members are close together. [Hierarchical radiosity](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/rad/) · [Wavelet radiosity](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/wavrad/)

## Measuring it

The [continuous motion benchmark](http://127.0.0.1:8736/motion.html) is the primary speed measurement. Choose one slat, two independent slats or two full blinds. A camera and its shadow-casting body travel together while the selected slats rotate and a separate solid moves near the bench. In the single-slat and two-slat replays, the radii also vary. Each of 120 successive 60 Hz simulation ticks goes through the normal scene API, equilibrium solve, camera update, full 4K draw and GPU readback. The page reports sustained finished frames per second, frame-time percentiles, missed work budgets and cold start. Expand **Mean moving-frame profile** for the additive breakdown through readback and the overlapping CPU work inside the exchange stage. A second pass advances game time at 60 Hz regardless of rendering speed and reports how many poses were shown or skipped. The two-second trajectory is identical on all backends. Choose four or eight additional moving solids to stress overlapping silhouettes and local contact shadows.

When scheduling support is available, the renderer gives the browser a chance to commit a submitted draw before beginning the next geometry update. The performance target is **1 ms for a complete moving frame at 4K**. The game clock still advances at 60 Hz. **GPU-resident contact field** is the play and benchmark default. The CPU clips visibility footprints. The GPU integrates the visible polygons into sixteen angular sectors and stores the area and source-gradient moment of each stationary aperture. Moving blockers trigger new GPU integrals only where their footprint changes visibility. The host uploads changed vertices in surface coordinates. As soon as visibility is ready, the GPU integrates those polygons while the CPU solves global lighting. It keeps the directional opening at each receiver and sums the area and first moment over directions for each diffuse source at a display site. Final shading multiplies those two coefficients by the current source RGB mean and slope. A material edit or lamp color change at fixed geometry reuses the result. A newly visible chart computes its response before it is drawn. Larger convex inputs select a larger GPU clipping kernel. This path requires WebGL 2 floating-point color buffers and blending. **CPU reference** and the historical **GPU with readback reference** are explicit benchmark comparisons. The page reports GPU sector submissions, uploads and peak contact buffer and texture payload. [Motion scene](src/motion-benchmark.js) · [Contact geometry](src/contact-transfer.js) · [GPU integral](src/gpu-contact.js) · [GPU lighting](src/resident-contacts.js)

To measure beyond readback, run the local server and launch a separate windowed Chrome with a fresh profile and remote debugging enabled on port 9222. With Python packages `websocket-client` and `Pillow` installed, run `python3 scripts/measure-presentation.py resident 4`. Select `cpu`, `gpu` or `resident` and 0, 4 or 8 extra solids. Add `--slats 1` or `--slats 2` to measure the individual slats. The script opens a benchmark tab, marks each finished 4K frame, matches captures to simulation ticks and reports start-to-swap median/p95 plus any missing pose IDs. Browser capture and marker drawing add overhead. The endpoint is Chrome’s frame-swap timestamp. Physical panel scanout requires a separate measurement. [Swap probe](scripts/measure-presentation.py) · [Chrome frame timestamp](https://chromedevtools.github.io/devtools-protocol/tot/Page/#type-ScreencastFrameMetadata)

Select **3840 × 2160** and enable **Time GPU completion**. **Scene update** measures wall time from preparation start through visible-field reconstruction, camera visibility, upload and draw submission. **Finished frame** includes synchronous GPU readback. **Last edit to frame** starts at the input handler. Browser compositing and physical refresh are outside those timings. Expand **Last scene update profile** for the CPU breakdown. The worker sweep overlaps lamp visibility and lamp integration, so the displayed stages cannot simply be added. Scanline visibility can overlap the lighting solve. The work list records prior-revision timings, independent light probes, whole-image comparisons and remaining failures. [Room benchmark results](OPEN_WORK.md#propagated-spatial-variation-and-sparse-exchange)

For an independent estimate of indirect radiance, run `node scripts/probe-light.mjs` for the bench underside or `node scripts/probe-light.mjs floor 2 2 back 24 262144` for a floor region. The arguments are surface id, two zero-based area indices, side, blind angle in degrees and path count. The probe reports linear RGB and standard errors using the mathematical fillet. It runs independently of the renderer. A final JSON argument accepts ordinary room edits. Reproduce the two-slat floor probe with `node scripts/probe-light.mjs floor 2 2 back 17.188733853924695 131072 '{"slats":1,"radius":0.044,"secondBlind":true,"secondRadius":0.033,"secondAngle":-0.4}'`. Authored angles in JSON are radians.

The checked-in WebAssembly modules run on the CPU. The moving contact visibility pass compiles each chart’s fixed apertures and receiver positions once. It clips the current solid silhouettes in reusable memory and writes visible polygon coordinates directly in the GPU’s input format. The GPU evaluates their angular integrals. [Visibility kernel](src/contact-visibility.c)

Rebuild the modules with Zig from the repository root.

```sh
zig cc -target wasm32-freestanding -O3 -c src/beam-sweep.c -o /tmp/room-beam.o
zig wasm-ld --strip-debug --no-entry --export=sweep --export=mergeLinks --export=openOperator --export=installExact --export=prepareRows --export=solveLinks --export=__heap_base --export-memory /tmp/room-beam.o -o src/beam-sweep.wasm
zig cc -target wasm32-freestanding -O3 -c src/contact-visibility.c -o /tmp/aspect-visibility.o
zig wasm-ld --strip-debug --no-entry --export=coneBytes --export=contactPackets --export=__heap_base --export-memory /tmp/aspect-visibility.o -o src/contact-visibility.wasm
zig cc -target wasm32-freestanding -O3 -c src/fillet-kernel.c -o /tmp/aspect-fillet.o
zig wasm-ld --strip-debug --no-entry --export=renderRuns --export=surfaceExchange --export=directSites --export=recoverFlux --export=__heap_base --export-memory -z stack-size=262144 /tmp/aspect-fillet.o -o src/fillet-kernel.wasm
```

## Two-tuft fur study

With the local server running, open [the fur study](http://127.0.0.1:8736/fur-lab/). **Brush center part** reproduces the measured groom. Drag in **Brush** mode to make your own part. Orbit and zoom, then switch between the CPU appearance, native-pixel individual hairs, and their difference. **Measure at 4K** takes the current groom on a repeatable twelve-view walk and exports per-view costs and image errors. The GPU displays CPU-generated images. The study lives in `fur-lab/` and can also run directly by opening its `index.html` in a WebGL 2 browser.
