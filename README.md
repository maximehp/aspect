# 3D vector engine

A walkable room with one point lamp and independently tiltable blinds. Run `npm start` and open [the room](http://127.0.0.1:8736/). The server uses Python 3. The browser needs hardware-accelerated WebGL 2 and floating-point render targets. There are no package dependencies.

Drag to look and use WASD to walk. Try rotating the blind slowly, then move the lamp slider to zero. Choose **Reflected only** and switch the floor between red and cobalt to see the color of the floor on the plaster. You can also add a second blind, tilt it independently, or rearrange the furniture.

## Authoring the room

The room uses planar surfaces, rounded boxes and linear RGB material reflectances. Lengths are in metres. A rectangle has a center and two half-axes. A rounded box has a center, three orthonormal axes, three outer half-extents and a fillet radius. Each slat is one solid 4 m long, 226 mm wide and 24 mm thick with 8 mm fillets. The same surface defines its silhouette, shading normal, shadow and diffuse reflections. Shapes are immutable. Submit a replacement shape to move it or change its material. ## How a frame gets its light

The lamp supplies all the energy. For direct light, receiver polygons encode which stationary planar surfaces can see the lamp. A conservative angular lookup table narrows the list of candidate blockers. The renderer intersects the moving solids and filters their projected shadow boundaries over each pixel. It uses the GPU rasterizer for coverage and solves curved slat hits inside their bounding boxes. [View renderer](src/live-renderer.js) · [Lamp visibility](src/lamp-visibility.js)

For reflected light, the GPU traces diffuse paths through the current geometry at sample locations on the surfaces. It combines directions sampled at the receiver with connections to illuminated points sampled from the lamp. Every bounce filters the light by the hit material's reflectance. Weighted random termination allows arbitrarily many bounces. The renderer interpolates the resulting incident light in world space and applies the visible material. It places extra sample locations near contact edges and the outlines of close parallel surfaces, including the picture on the wall. Where the spacing changes, the renderer splits the presentation polygons at those coordinates so the GPU can interpolate the lookup across each visible region. The spacing comes from the scene geometry. Rotating a blind reuses the stationary surfaces’ coordinates. The earlier patch-exchange experiment remains in the repository for reference. The running room loads the GPU transport module through its view renderer. [Diffuse transport](src/live-lighting.js) · [Surface field](src/irradiance-layout.js)

A geometry edit gets its first 16 paths per location on the frame that shows the new geometry. Subsequent frames refine that pose to 1,024 paths per location. For small movements, matching random paths at a previously refined pose help cancel noise in the new estimate. A spatial filter reduces fluctuations on the early frames and fades out. Narrow indirect lighting features can be lost between sample locations even after refinement. In particular, the small cavity behind the picture still receives too much blue indirect light in the reference comparison. [Paired estimates](src/paired-history.js)

Moving the camera reuses the world-space field. Moving the dimmer scales both direct and indirect at full floating-point shading precision before tone mapping. Zero power clears the frame to black. Once the field has reached its sample budget, walking and dimming require no new paths. Refinement pauses with the lamp off or in direct-only mode.

## Measuring it

Choose **3840 × 2160** to render a full 4K framebuffer regardless of the size of the on-screen canvas. **Scene revisions shown** counts geometry or material revisions drawn. **Reflection samples** is the current path count per field location. **Scene update** measures the CPU time spent preparing and issuing a scene upload in that frame. Enable **Time GPU completion** for the time from the start of the draw callback through a synchronous one-pixel GPU readback. **Last edit to frame** starts in the geometry or material input handler and includes the wait for the draw callback. Both timings end before browser compositing or display refresh. Readback itself can affect frame pacing.

For an independent estimate of indirect light under the bench, run `node scripts/probe-light.mjs`. This CPU probe samples positions throughout the specified area, follows diffuse paths against the room's planes and filleted solids, and reports mean linear RGB radiance, standard errors, and the first and later reflected contributions. Choose a different region with `node scripts/probe-light.mjs floor 2 2 back 24 262144`. The arguments are the surface id, two zero-based area indices, side, blind angle in degrees and path count. The front normal is the cross product of the surface's half-axes. The area indices identify measurement regions used in earlier comparisons. [Run records and remaining work](OPEN_WORK.md)
